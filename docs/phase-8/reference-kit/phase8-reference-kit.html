Principles

  1. Physics truth and look truth are different jobs. Physics gets measured against numbers with a tolerance. Looks get judged side by side and then blind. Don't let a pretty frame hide a wrong number, or a right number excuse an ugly frame.
  2. Every reference carries its conditions. Wind speed and direction, sun position, sea state, camera and lens. A clip without known wind is mood reference only.
  3. Reproduce, then compare. A game shot is only compared with a real shot taken under the same conditions and camera.
  4. J/22 on Lake Ray Hubbard is the anchor. It is the boat and lake Bean actually sails, and Bean confirmed going deep on it first. The other seven boats get a lighter pass, focused on the known gaps.
  5. Wrong habits outrank wrong pixels. A gap that would teach Bean something false on the water (helm feel, heel, when to reef) ranks above a cosmetic one.

Selection rules:

  • Prefer clips with stated wind, or races where a day report gives the wind (the 2024 J/22 Worlds days are ideal).
  • For motion measurement, avoid gimbal-stabilised or warped (fisheye, horizon-leveled) footage. An unstabilised action cam on a rigid mount is best.
  • For colour, avoid graded, slow-motion or sunset-filtered footage. Use webcams and Bean's own phone photos.
  • When wind is not stated, estimate it from the water using the NOAA Beaufort descriptions: scattered whitecaps start at Force 3 (7-10 kn), many whitecaps at Force 4 (11-16 kn). Mark it as estimated.
  • Pair Bean's own photos and clips with station wind at the same minute: Mesquite (KHQZ, 5.6 mi from the club) and Rockwall (F46, 8.2 mi) through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet ASOS download.

  • true wind speed, direction, gust factor; sea-state seed

Looks, judged side by side

Put each matched pair in one image (real left, game right) and score these from 1 (same) to 5 (obviously wrong):

  • Water: colour for the venue (Ray Hubbard and Galveston Bay are turbid olive-green to tan; Gulf offshore bluer), chop scale and steepness, foam and whitecap coverage, sun glint, cat's paws, reflections.
  • Sky: haze near the horizon, cloud types, storm darkness (shelf cloud and rain curtain), sun disc and glare, night sky and moon.
  • Sails: depth, twist, telltales, luffing, wrinkles, leech flutter, translucency against the sun.
  • Boat: heel posture, crew position, waterline, bow wave, spray, wake.
  • Motion: camera shake and horizon roll for onboard views; pitching in chop.
  • Sound (from Bean's recordings): luff flutter, water along the hull, wind noise against speed, winches, halyard slap at the dock.

Also run NVIDIA FLIP on matched stills as a rough difference number. It is only a trend signal, because real and game frames will never align pixel for pixel.

Step 4: Turn differences into a ranked fix list

Score every gap:

Priority = Severity (1-5) × Visibility (1-5) × Teaching impact (1-3) ÷ Cost (1-5)

  • Severity: how far off it is (5 = clearly wrong to a sailor).
  • Visibility: how often it shows up in a normal session on the home lake.
  • Teaching impact: 3 if it would teach a wrong habit or wrong expectation, 1 if purely cosmetic.
  • Cost: rough effort to fix.

Work the list top down. After each fix, recapture the same reference scenes, rescore, and save the new frame as a "golden" screenshot so later changes can't quietly undo it.

Step 5: Blind test

Make 20 stills (10 real, 10 game, matched conditions), shuffled. Bean (and anyone else who sails) marks each real or game and write one line on why. Every "why" for a correctly spotted game frame becomes a fix-list item. Goal: game frames spotted under 70% of the time by the end of Phase 8. Repeat once after fixes.

Step 6: Bean's own data (best references)

Bean has no lesson media yet, so these start from the next lesson. With the instructor's OK:

  • Phone logger. Mount the phone rigidly (cockpit bulkhead, not a pocket). Run Sensor Logger or phyphox at 10 Hz or more: GPS, accelerometer, gyroscope, orientation. Note crew, sail plan, start and stop times. This gives real heel, roll period, tacking angle and speed at known wind.
  • Roll-decay test at the dock: rock the boat sharply once, let it settle, log it. Gives the real roll period and damping.
  • Helm feel. Tape a phone to the tiller or film the tiller from above for a minute upwind in steady wind. Gives real weather-helm angle for the light weather helm gap.
  • Shot list: helm view upwind and downwind; boat from the dock at the slip; sky every hour; water close-up; the marina from the end of the dock; 30-second sound clips (luffing, sailing upwind, at the dock).
  • Scans. J/22 and Rush Creek docks, 150-250 overlapping photos in rings at two heights, overcast light if possible. Process with Apple Object Capture on the Mac or RealityScan. Bean's iPhone is a Pro, so use a LiDAR scan app (for example Polycam or RealityScan's LiDAR mode) for the docks and the photo method for the hull.
  • Colour. One photo per session that includes something of known colour (white sail, a grey card if Bean has one).

2. Sunfish

Published data points / behavior notes (Sunfish)

  • Capsize behavior: a Sunfish does not self-right. After a capsize it floats on its side with the lateen sail flat on the water. If the sail is left there, it can turtle with the mast pointing down, and in shallow lakes the mast can stick in mud. To recover, the sailor swims to the daggerboard, which must stay fully down, pulls or stands on it, and the boat comes up, usually pointing head to wind. The Lee Montes clip below covers how to avoid turtling.
  • Turtling: a capsized dinghy that is not righted promptly can roll from about 90° to fully inverted. A float on the masthead prevents this. Climbing onto the high side of a knocked-down boat raises weight and windage and makes turtling more likely. Righting from turtle means standing on the centreboard. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turtling_(sailing)
  • 2023 Sunfish Worlds, Miami (5-9 Dec 2023): Day 1 had a strong NW wind gusting into the 20s. Days 2-5 had gusts to about 12 kn. Steep chop made upwind work hard, and offwind conditions gave "near-ideal surfing". Source: https://www.sailingscuttlebutt.com/?p=123413

Sim check: in a capsize the Sunfish should settle at about 90° and stay there until the sailor applies a righting moment on the daggerboard. Any sim auto-righting is unrealistic. When it comes up it often swings head to wind and can capsize again to windward if the sheet is cleated. The low freeboard means water sweeps over the deck in chop.

3. ILCA 7 / Laser

Published data points (ILCA/Laser)

  • Polars and heel (Day et al. 2017, "Performance prediction for sailing dinghies", Ocean Engineering, Strathclyde): https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/59980/1/Day_OE_2017_Performance_prediction_for_sailing_dinghies.pdf
    • Measured Laser polars exist at 9 and 12 kn TWS (Binns et al. 2002) and from an instrumented GPS boat at about 12.5 kn (Clark 2014).
    • Best upwind VMG falls at TWA about 40-46° in flat water. Apparent wind angle runs about 27° at 5 kn to about 33° at 15 kn.
    • Upwind, the boat is sailed flat up to about 9 kn TWS, then with a small heel.
    • Downwind in light air, the typical windward heel is about 20° (by the lee, "kiting"). The VPP finds the optimum heel switching abruptly at about 9.1 kn TWS.
    • The text gives no numeric speeds (they are in figures only).
  • Chalmers study: best heel and trim of a Laser upwind at 2-5 kn of boat speed. It finds a small heel is beneficial in higher winds. https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/234579
  • Anecdotal top speed: 15 kn GPS on a broad reach in a "very big gust", including about 2 kn of tide (forum post): https://www.yachtsandyachting.com/FORUM/printer_friendly_posts.asp?TID=13885

Key realism takeaways for the sim developer

  1. Water colour. DFW reservoirs (Ray Hubbard, Lewisville) and Galveston Bay are turbid. Aim for olive-green to tan-brown, with little light reaching the bottom and a short absorption depth. Lake Travis is the clear-water outlier. Calibrate against entries 58, 62, 86 and 116.
  2. Wave spectrum. Ray Hubbard is a fetch-limited reservoir: max depth 12 m and only a few km of fetch. Expect short, steep chop of about 0.3-0.6 m in 20 kn, with periods of 2-3 s and no swell. Galveston Bay is also shallow (~2-3 m), so its chop is short and steep. The Gulf adds swell of 5-8 s.
  3. Whitecaps start appearing around 12-15 kn (entry 109).
  4. Hobie 16 events. A pitchpole starts with the leeward bow burying in a bear-away or a gust. It is a diagonal pitch-roll, not a clean end-over-end (entries 18-22). Righting takes 1-3 minutes and needs a righting line plus crew weight (entries 24-26).
  5. Trimarans. A trimaran heels ~10-15° on the leeward ama and cannot be righted by its crew. Use F-28 clips for the heavy-air band (entries 34-37).
  6. Lagoon 42. Heel is minimal and it slams under the bridgedeck in chop. It points poorly, and its high windage makes it drift quickly at the dock (entries 42 and 49-51).
  7. North Texas storms. An arriving shelf cloud brings a sudden wind shift, gusts of 40+ kn, a rain curtain and lightning. The chop can build in minutes, as in the NBC DFW article (entry 66).
  8. Kelvin wake. Arms sit at 19.47°, narrowing at high Froude number (entries 105-106). Ship-channel wakes run across Galveston Bay as long-period waves (entry 100).

  • Sunfish: the hull is a sealed hollow pontoon, so it floats when capsized ("self-rescuing", not self-righting) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunfish_(sailboat)). No official Sunfish manual text on righting was retrievable. The Vanguard rigging manual (https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1739245/Vanguard-Sunfish.html) and the MYC rigging manual (https://myc.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Sunfish-Riggin-Manual.pdf) don't cover it.
  • General dinghy procedure (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsizing): stand on or pull the centreboard/daggerboard to lift the mast, and "the hull's righting moment usually takes over once the mast is about 30 degrees from horizontal". Point the bow into the wind so the wind helps lift the sail, and avoid a re-capsize to the other side. Dinghies are treated as non-self-righting, and they can turtle (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turtling_(sailing)). Recovery from turtle: release sheets, stand on the hull or board, lever.
  • Hobie 16: stand on the lower hull and pull a righting line over the upper hull. Act immediately, or it may turtle and be very hard to right.
  • Discover Boating capsize recovery (scoop and single-handed): https://www.discoverboating.com/resources/sailing-capsize-recovery-scoop-and-single-handed-methods
  • US Sailing Dinghy Entrapment report: https://www.ussailing.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Dinghy-Entrapment-Report_Final.pdf
  • Sim rule: Sunfish, ILCA, and Hobie 16 must stay capsized (stable at ~90°, able to turtle) until the player applies a righting action (board/line). Keelboats (J/22, C22, C30) self-right. Corsair 880 and Lagoon 42 are not self-righting once inverted, but they have very high initial stability.

4.2 Beaufort / sea state / whitecaps

  • NOAA Guide to Sea State, Wind and Clouds: https://www.weather.gov/media/marine/SeaState.pdf. Also https://www.wpc.ncep.noaa.gov/html/beaufort.shtml and https://www.weather.gov/pqr/Beaufort
  • Key rows: F2 4–6 kt small wavelets, glassy crests, not breaking. F3 7–10 kt crests begin to break, scattered whitecaps (whitecap onset ~7–10 kt). F4 11–16 kt numerous whitecaps. F5 17–21 kt many whitecaps, some spray. F6 22–27 kt whitecaps everywhere. F7 28–33 kt foam blown in streaks. Probable wave heights in the table (F3 0.6–1 m, F4 1–1.5 m) are open-ocean values. A fetch-limited lake is far lower (see §4.1). Use the visual descriptors (whitecap coverage, streaks) and the §4.1 heights.
  • How to use: Drive the foam/whitecap coverage parameter from wind speed. It should be zero below ~7 kt and ramp through F3–F6. Check against reference photos taken at known logged wind speed (§5).

4.3 Kelvin wake

  • https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin_wake_pattern: half-angle 19.47° = arcsin(1/3) (total ~39°), deep water, independent of speed at moderate Froude number. At high hull Froude number Fr = U/√(gL) (Rabaud & Moisy 2013), the apparent wake angle narrows. In shallow water (depth Froude U/√(gh) → 1) the wake widens toward 90°.
  • How to use: Check that the sim wake arms sit at ~19.5° for a displacement keelboat. Transverse wavelength λ = 2πU²/g (e.g. 6 kt → ~9.5 m, physics-standard). Narrower apparent wakes are expected for planing dinghies and cats.

4.4 Heel, righting moment, roll period

  • Static heel: RM(φ) = Δ·g·GZ(φ) with GZ ≈ GM·sin φ at small angles. Equilibrium where heeling moment from sails (F_side × CE height above CLR) = RM(φ) + crew hiking moment. Standard references: Larsson & Eliasson, Principles of Yacht Design [book, no URL], and the ORC VPP documentation at https://orc.org [not fetched].
  • Roll period (IMO Res. A.562(14) weather criterion): T = 2·C·B / √GM, with C = 0.373 + 0.023 (B/d) − 0.043 (L/100) (L, B, d, GM in metres): https://wwwcdn.imo.org/localresources/en/KnowledgeCentre/IndexofIMOResolutions/AssemblyDocuments/A.562(14).pdf. Ships formula, so use it only as a sanity check. J/22 example (derived): B 2.446, d 1.179, L 6.86, GM 1.06 gives C 0.418 and T ≈ 2.0 s. Compare with the phone-measured roll decay (§5.2), since the free roll period of a small keelboat should come out ~2–3 s. Physics form: T = 2π k / √(g·GM), k = roll radius of gyration ≈ 0.35–0.45 B.

6. Suggested calibration checklist (how the above fits together)

  1. Wind climate: fetch HQZ + F46 (5-min), DAL (1-min gusts), Eagle Point (6-min overwater). Fit seasonal speed, direction, and gust-factor distributions.
  2. Waves: Ray Hubbard uses directional fetch × §4.1 (Hs 0.2–1 m, Tp 1.5–3.5 s for 10–25 kt). Galveston Bay uses SPM-shallow with d ≈ 2 m. The Gulf uses JONSWAP fits to 42035 spectra.
  3. Boat speed: J/22 against the ORC table. C30 against keelindex/ORC. ILCA against Day (2017). Cross-boat ratios against D-PN per wind band. Corsair 880 against the MW185 spot points. Fill Hobie and Lagoon from your own GPS logs.
  4. Heel and roll: J/22 3–4° heel at 6–8 kt per North. GM ≈ 1.06 m from ORC RM. Roll period ~2 s (IMO estimate), validated by phone roll-decay tests.
  5. Capsize: dinghies and the beach cat stay down until righted. Keelboats self-right.
  6. Visuals: whitecap onset at F3 (7–10 kt). Kelvin half-angle 19.47°. Sky checked against Kider measured radiance. Exposure in physical units. Moon from USNO. Reference photos with ColorChecker, grey and chrome balls, and HDRI, compared via ꟻLIP.
