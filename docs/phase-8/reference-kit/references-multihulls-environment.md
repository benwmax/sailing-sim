# Reference Footage: Multihulls + Texas/Gulf Environment (realism pass)

Compiled 2026-10-12. Every URL below came back from a live web search during this session; no video IDs were invented.

**Verification status:**
- **[S]**: found in search results. The title and URL come from the search index, but nobody watched the page. YouTube fetches were rate-limited (HTTP 429) during this session, so most videos carry this tag.
- **[V]**: the page was fetched with WebFetch and its content was confirmed.
- Conditions in brackets come from the video title. Treat them as claims, and check them against what you see in the footage.

Wind bands: **LIGHT** 0-7 kn, **MODERATE** 8-15, **HEAVY** 16-25, **STORM** 25+ / capsize / pitchpole / broach.

Reference specs for sanity checks:
- **Hobie 16** [V], from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobie_16
  - LOA 5.05 m, beam 2.41 m, hull weight 145 kg, sail area 20 m².
- **Lake Ray Hubbard** [V], from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ray_Hubbard
  - 22,745 acres (~92 km²), max depth 12 m (40 ft).
  - Shallow and fetch-limited, so expect short, steep chop rather than swell.
- **Kelvin wake** [V], from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin_wake_pattern
  - Each arm sits 19.47° off the track (arcsin 1/3), for a full V of ~39°.
  - The individual wavelets lie at ~53° to the track.

## Key realism takeaways for the sim developer

1. **Water colour.** DFW reservoirs (Ray Hubbard, Lewisville) and Galveston Bay are turbid. Aim for olive-green to tan-brown, with little light reaching the bottom and a short absorption depth. Lake Travis is the clear-water outlier. Calibrate against entries 58, 62, 86 and 116.
2. **Wave spectrum.** Ray Hubbard is a fetch-limited reservoir: max depth 12 m and only a few km of fetch. Expect short, steep chop of about 0.3-0.6 m in 20 kn, with periods of 2-3 s and no swell. Galveston Bay is also shallow (~2-3 m), so its chop is short and steep. The Gulf adds swell of 5-8 s.
3. **Whitecaps** start appearing around 12-15 kn (entry 109).
4. **Hobie 16 events.** A pitchpole starts with the leeward bow burying in a bear-away or a gust. It is a diagonal pitch-roll, not a clean end-over-end (entries 18-22). Righting takes 1-3 minutes and needs a righting line plus crew weight (entries 24-26).
5. **Trimarans.** A trimaran heels ~10-15° on the leeward ama and **cannot be righted by its crew**. Use F-28 clips for the heavy-air band (entries 34-37).
6. **Lagoon 42.** Heel is minimal and it slams under the bridgedeck in chop. It points poorly, and its high windage makes it drift quickly at the dock (entries 42 and 49-51).
7. **North Texas storms.** An arriving shelf cloud brings a sudden wind shift, gusts of 40+ kn, a rain curtain and lightning. The chop can build in minutes, as in the NBC DFW article (entry 66).
8. **Kelvin wake.** Arms sit at 19.47°, narrowing at high Froude number (entries 105-106). Ship-channel wakes run across Galveston Bay as long-period waves (entry 100).
